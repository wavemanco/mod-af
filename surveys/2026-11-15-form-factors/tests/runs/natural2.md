# Decision brief: moving weekly planning from Monday morning to Thursday afternoon

## The decision
You're deciding when the team agrees what it will work on each week. The timing affects whether people start the week already knowing their priorities or spend Monday working them out. It's easy to undo: if it doesn't work, you change a calendar invite.

## Options

**1. Keep Monday morning**
- **What it is:** No change. The team plans at the start of the week it is planning.
- **Pros:** Everyone has the freshest information, including anything that happened over the weekend. No adjustment period.
- **Cons and risks:** Monday morning usually goes to catching up on email, so planning can feel rushed. Monday holidays push planning to Tuesday, especially in the US (Memorial Day, Labor Day and others). People can't start real work until the meeting ends. If this is wrong, you've just stayed where you are.
- **Cost:** None.

**2. Move to Thursday afternoon**
- **What it is:** The team plans next week near the end of this one. Friday is for finishing current work and preparing.
- **Pros:** People arrive on Monday knowing what to do. You get a day to resolve blockers and dependencies before the new week starts. Monday holidays no longer get in the way. Last week's results are still fresh when you plan.
- **Cons and risks:** Late Thursday is often a low-energy slot. Anything that changes on Friday or over the weekend can make part of the plan stale. If some people have Fridays off or work compressed weeks, it may suit them less. The cost if it's wrong is small: a few weeks of awkward meetings and then you switch back.
- **Cost:** A calendar change and one week where the cadence shifts. The last Monday meeting and the first Thursday one cover overlapping ground.

**3. Middle ground: plan in writing on Thursday, short check on Monday**
- **What it is:** Everyone writes their plan for next week by Thursday. On Monday the team meets for 15 minutes to confirm it and adjust.
- **Pros:** You get Thursday's head start and Monday's fresh information. The Monday meeting is short.
- **Cons and risks:** It only works if people actually write their plans. You keep two touchpoints, and it can drift back into a full Monday meeting.
- **Cost:** A little more process to set up and keep going.

## Recommendation
**Move to Thursday afternoon (option 2) as a 4-week trial, with a quick check at the end.**

The main reason to plan is so people know what to do when the week starts. A Monday meeting delays that every week. Because the change is cheap and easy to reverse, a trial is the sensible way to find out. Schedule it early in the afternoon, around 1–2pm, not at 4pm when people are tired. At the end of the trial, ask the team two questions:
- Did Mondays start faster?
- Did the plan survive the weekend?

If the answers are mixed, try option 3 next.

## What would change this
- **If your work changes a lot between Thursday and Monday** (weekend customer issues, new priorities landing on Fridays, a client who sets direction on Monday), Thursday plans will often be out of date. Keep Monday, or use option 3.
- **If several people are off on Fridays or work different time zones**, Thursday afternoon may be a worse slot for them than Monday morning. Check who would actually be available before you switch.
