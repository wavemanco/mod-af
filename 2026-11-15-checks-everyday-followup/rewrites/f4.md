# School Bus Crash: Bus Left the Road and Rolled Over

Monaville, Texas

December 17, 2021

## What Happened

The crash happened on Friday, December 17, 2021, at about 12:10 p.m.

The bus belonged to Hempstead Independent School District (ISD). An ISD is a local public school district in Texas.

The bus was a 2018 model made by IC Bus. It could carry 43 passengers. Its seats had lap belts. A lap belt is a seat belt that goes across the hips only.

The bus was heading south on Farm to Market Road 1887. This is a rural road in Monaville, in Waller County, Texas. The speed limit was 55 miles per hour (mph). The weather was clear. The road was dry.

Six people were on the bus:

- the driver, age 59
- a bus aide, age 59 (an aide is an adult helper who rides with students)
- four students

The bus crossed a bridge near Holik Road. Then it drifted off the right side of its lane. The driver turned the wheel left, then right, then left again. She lost control. The bus left the road. It rolled over one full time.

## Who Was Hurt

- The aide was thrown out of the bus. The aide died.
- The driver had minor injuries.
- Three students had minor injuries.
- One student was in a child safety seat. That student was not hurt.

## Why It Happened

The National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) looked into this crash. The NTSB is a U.S. government agency. It studies crashes to find out why they happen.

The NTSB found the main cause. The driver was distracted by the bus's vent window. A vent window is a small window that opens to let in air. Because of this distraction, the bus left its lane.

Injuries were worse because several people were not wearing their seat belts.

## Seat Belts on School Buses

School buses are one of the safest ways to travel. The NTSB works to make them even safer. Wearing seat belts adds more safety.

Texas law required the school district to have a rule that riders wear seat belts. At the time of the crash, the district did not have this rule.

Since the crash, the district has made changes:

- Students and staff must now wear seat belts.
- The district hired more staff. They keep track of bus repairs. They also check that drivers meet job rules.

Many buses have cameras inside. The NTSB says schools should check this video from time to time. This helps make sure riders stay seated and wear their belts.

The NTSB says a seat belt rule and video checks would likely have helped. More riders would have been seated and buckled.

**If everyone had worn the lap belts, injuries would have been less severe. No one would have been thrown from the bus.**

## Safety Technology That Could Have Helped

Newer vehicles can have tools that help the driver. Three such tools could have helped in this crash.

**Lane departure warning.** This system uses cameras to watch the lines on the road. It beeps and flashes when the vehicle starts to drift out of its lane. It might have pulled the driver's attention back to the road.

**Lane departure prevention.** This system also warns the driver. But it goes one step further. It can steer the vehicle back into its lane by itself. It might have kept the bus on the road in the first place.

**Electronic stability control (ESC).** This system helps when a vehicle starts to slide or skid. It can brake single wheels. It can also ease off the engine power. This helps stop the vehicle from spinning sideways. After the bus left its lane, the driver turned the wheel very hard. ESC might have kept the bus steady. It might have stopped the rollover.

The bus in this crash did not have any of these systems.

## What Has Changed Since Then

The NTSB has asked the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) to act. NHTSA is the U.S. agency that sets safety rules for vehicles. The NTSB has asked NHTSA to:

- require lane departure prevention on large work vehicles over 10,000 pounds, including school buses
- set clear standards for how well ESC must work on these vehicles

IC Bus is the company that made this bus. It now puts ESC on all its new school buses. This started in late 2018. Lane warning and lane-keeping features are not standard. But schools can choose to add them to new buses.

## Questions a Parent Might Ask the School

- Does our district require students to wear seat belts on the bus?
- Do staff check bus video to see if students stay buckled?
- Do our buses have ESC or lane warning systems?
