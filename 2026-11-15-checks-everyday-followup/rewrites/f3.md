# Section 20: The Hospital Stay Your Parent Needs Before Medicare Pays for Nursing Home Care

*(Revision 1, October 1, 2003. Old references: A3-3131, SNF-212.)*

A skilled nursing facility (SNF) is a nursing home that gives daily nursing or therapy care. Medicare Part A can pay for a short SNF stay after a hospital stay. Two rules must be met first.

1. **The hospital stay rule.** Your parent must have been a hospital inpatient for at least 3 days in a row. The stay must have been medically needed.
2. **The 30-day rule.** Your parent must move to the SNF within 30 days of leaving the hospital. The SNF must take part in Medicare. This rule has applied since December 5, 1980. There is one exception, described in Section 20.2.

## 20.1: The 3-Day Hospital Stay

*(Revision 10880. Issued August 6, 2021. Effective November 8, 2021.)*

### When the hospital stay must happen

The hospital discharge must happen on or after a certain date. That date is the first day of the month when your parent:

- turned 65, or
- became eligible for Medicare because of a disability or kidney failure. (This option began July 1, 1973.)

This comes from section 226(c)(1)(B) of the Social Security Act. It also comes from federal rule 42 CFR 409.30(a)(2).

### How the days are counted

- The 3 days must be in a row.
- The days can be spread across more than one hospital.
- The day your parent is admitted counts.
- The day your parent leaves does **not** count.

**Example:** Your parent is admitted Monday and leaves Thursday. Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday count. That is 3 days, so the rule is met.

### Observation and emergency room time do not count

An "inpatient" is a person the hospital has formally admitted. Some patients stay in the hospital without being admitted. This can happen in two ways:

- **Emergency room (ER) time.** Your parent is seen or treated in the ER.
- **Observation.** Observation means the hospital watches your parent to decide whether to admit them.

In both cases your parent is an "outpatient." An outpatient is someone who gets hospital care without being admitted. This is true even if they stay overnight. Outpatient time does not count toward the 3 days.

The count starts on the day your parent is formally admitted as an inpatient.

**Tip:** Ask the hospital staff whether your parent is "inpatient" or "under observation." Ask more than once during the stay.

### The SNF care must relate to the hospital stay

Medicare pays for SNF care only if it treats one of these:

- a condition your parent was treated for in the hospital, or
- a new condition that started in the SNF while they were being treated there for the hospital condition.

The condition does not have to be the main reason your parent went to the hospital. It can be any condition they had during the hospital stay.

### The hospital stay must have been medically needed

Medicare usually assumes the hospital stay was needed.

Sometimes a claim raises doubts. Then the Medicare Administrative Contractor (MAC) looks into it. A MAC is the company that handles Medicare claims in your parent's area. The MAC may talk with your parent's doctor and the hospital.

The MAC will say the stay was not needed only in one case. That case is when a 3-day stay is far outside normal medical practice.

There is one more case to know about. Sometimes Medicare protects a patient from paying for care that turned out not to be covered. This protection is called "limitation on liability." It applies when the patient could not have known the care would not be covered. If your parent got this protection for any part of the hospital stay, that part counts as not medically needed. That part then cannot count toward the 3 days. (See the Medicare Claims Processing Manual, Chapter 30, Section 130.2.A.)

### Lighter care near the end of the stay

Your parent's care may become less intense near the end of the hospital stay. That is fine. The discharge date is the day your parent physically leaves the hospital. The rule is met if at least 3 days in a row were medically needed inpatient care.

### Waiting in the hospital for a nursing home bed

Sometimes a patient no longer needs hospital-level care. They now need nursing-home-level care. But no SNF bed is open yet.

In this case, a doctor can state in writing that staying in the hospital is medically needed. The days spent waiting are called "alternate placement" days. These days **do** count toward the 3 days. (See federal rule 42 CFR 424.13(c). Also see the Medicare General Information, Eligibility, and Entitlement Manual, Chapter 4, Section 10.6.)

### Which hospitals count

- The hospital does **not** need a special transfer agreement with the SNF.
- The hospital **must** take part in Medicare. Or it must meet Medicare's standards for an emergency services hospital.
- A psychiatric hospital does not need to take part in Medicare. It also does not need to meet Medicare's extra rules for psychiatric hospitals.
- Stays in a Religious Nonmedical Health Care Institution (RNHCI) do **not** count. An RNHCI gives care based on religious beliefs instead of medical treatment.
- A stay can also count if your parent was on hospice and got inpatient hospital care through hospice. Hospice is comfort care for people near the end of life. See the Medicare Benefit Policy Manual, Chapter 9, Section 40.1.5.

These sources have more detail: the Medicare General Information, Eligibility, and Entitlement Manual, Chapter 5, Sections 20.2, 20.3, 30.2 and 40.

### Note on psychiatric hospital stays

A 3-day stay in a psychiatric hospital does meet the hospital stay rule. But a facility that mainly gives psychiatric care cannot be a Medicare SNF.

So a patient may have only a mental health condition. If that patient moves from a psychiatric hospital to an SNF, Medicare will likely not pay for the SNF care.

"Non-covered care" means care that is less than the skilled level Medicare pays for. Section 30 and the sections after it explain this more.
